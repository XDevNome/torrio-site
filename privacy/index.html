PrivacyPrivacy Policy

For the Torrio app, its Google Drive and OneDrive integrations, and the Torrio Integration browser extension · Last updated 12 November 2026

In short: Torrio collects no personal data. There are no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. The browser extension only talks to the Torrio app on your own computer, and nothing it handles is sent to us or to anyone else. If you connect Google Drive or OneDrive, your files and account details go only between that service and your computer, never to us.

The Torrio Integration extension

The extension connects your browser to the Torrio app installed on the same computer, using the browser's built-in native messaging. It has no server of its own and sends nothing over the internet.

When you download something, the extension passes these details only to the Torrio app on your computer, so it can download the file the same way your browser would:

  • the address (URL) of the file or video, and the page it came from;
  • the file name, size and type the website reports;
  • the cookies and browser identification (user agent) for that website, because many sites only allow a download when they're included.

The extension doesn't read or store your browsing history, doesn't collect form data, passwords or messages, and doesn't sell, share or transfer any data. Its settings (such as whether it's on, and where you placed the video button) are kept in the browser's own storage on your computer.

The Torrio app

The app works entirely on your computer. Your downloads, download history, settings, music library and playlists stay on your computer. Torrio connects to the internet only to:

  • download what you ask it to, from the websites you choose;
  • check torrio.devnome.com for a new version;
  • download the free, open-source helper tools it uses (for video, conversion and playback) from their official public pages on GitHub, when you first need them or choose to get them;
  • talk to Google Drive or OneDrive, only if you sign in to them in Torrio (see below).

Like any website, these servers see your computer's internet address when it connects. We don't collect or keep it.

Google Drive

Coming soon. The Google Drive section isn't available in Torrio yet; this is how it will handle your data.

Torrio's Google Drive section lets you download your own files, files shared with you and shared links from Google Drive. It's off until you sign in with your Google account there; until then Torrio receives no Google data.

What Torrio accesses. When you sign in, Google asks you to allow Torrio to see and download all your Google Drive files (the read-only permission https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.readonly). With it, Torrio reads:

  • the names, sizes, types and dates of your files and folders, to show them to you in Torrio;
  • the contents of the files you choose to download;
  • your Google account name and email address, to show which account is signed in.

How it's used. Only to show you your Google Drive and to download the files you choose to the folder you choose on your computer. Torrio can't change, move, share or delete anything on your Google Drive.

Where it's kept. On your computer only. Your sign-in (a token from Google) is stored encrypted by Windows for your Windows account. While a download is in progress, the list of its files (names, sizes and Google's file ids) is kept in Torrio's settings folder so it can be paused and resumed; it's deleted when you remove the download. Downloaded files are kept where you saved them until you delete them.

Sharing. Google user data is never sent to DevNome or to anyone else, never sold, never used for advertising, and never used to develop, improve or train AI or machine-learning models. Torrio has no servers that receive it.

Removing access. Press Sign out in Torrio's Google Drive section to delete the saved sign-in from your computer, or remove Torrio at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Torrio's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

OneDrive

Torrio's OneDrive section works the same way for Microsoft accounts. It's off until you sign in there. When you do, Microsoft asks you to allow Torrio to read all files you can access (Files.Read.All, read-only), sign you in and read your profile (User.Read, for your name and email) and keep access (offline_access, so you don't have to sign in every hour).

Torrio uses this only to show you your OneDrive and shared links and to download the files you choose to your computer. Your sign-in is stored encrypted by Windows on your computer, and nothing is sent to DevNome or anyone else, sold, or used for advertising or AI training. Press Sign out in Torrio to delete it, or remove Torrio at account.live.com/consent/Manage (personal accounts) or myapps.microsoft.com (work or school accounts).
